README - Verification of the main results of Motor_sizing.m
=====================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This README contains MATLAB copy/paste commands to verify the main results of the script:
- Motor_sizing.m, for Parts A-E;

The script produces:
- Part A: Design parameters, thermochemistry, Vieille's law, nozzle;
- Part B: BATES grain geometry;
- Part C: Quasi-steady time-domain profiles: pressure, thrust, burning rate, impulse;
- Part D: Thermal protections and liner;
- Part E: Nozzle ablative protection;

MATLAB prerequisites
--------------------
- MATLAB R2025b



1. Nominal verification of Parts A-E
--------------------------------------------------------

Copy/paste in command window:

```
clear; close all; clc;
diary('Motor_sizing_full_output.txt');
run('Motor_sizing.m');
diary off;
```

This command:
- runs the script generating the A to E parts;
- saves the text output in `Motor_sizing_full_output.txt`;
- opens the two figures generated by the script;

3. Output files summary
--------------------------------------------

After running sections 1 and 2, the following files are generated in the working directory:
- Motor_sizing_full_output.txt: complete text output of the script (Parts A-E);
- Motor_sizing_main_results_summary.csv: summary table of the main nominal results;
- Figure 1: Design parameters and nozzle (Vieille's law fit, nozzle profile, Mach evolution);
- Figure 2: Time-domain profiles (thrust, pressure, burning rate, burning area, mass flow rates, total impulse);

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Motor_sizing graphs explanation

Figure 1 – Design Parameters & Nozzle
The first figure summarizes the main design parameters related to combustion and the nozzle. On the left, it 
shows the fit of Vieille's law, namely the relationship between chamber pressure and propellant 
regression rate: the points represent the experimental data, while the curve represents the fitted law. 
In the middle, the nozzle geometry is shown, including the convergent section, throat, and divergent section. 
On the right, the evolution of the Mach number along the nozzle is reported, which is useful to verify the transition 
from subsonic flow to sonic flow at the throat and then to supersonic flow in the divergent section.

Figure 2 – Time-domain profiles
The second figure describes the time evolution of the motor during the quasi-steady combustion of the BATES grain. 
It shows thrust, chamber pressure, regression rate, burning surface area, the mass flow rates generated by combustion 
and discharged through the nozzle, and finally the total impulse. This figure is therefore used to verify that the 
motor satisfies the main design requirements, especially thrust, maximum pressure, burning time, and total impulse.

Parts D and E produce text output only (no figures).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Motor_sizing code explanation

The script is a unified MATLAB preliminary design tool for the STARK-100k solid rocket motor. 
It starts by defining the main design requirements, namely the target thrust of 100 kN and the total impulse
of 2.5 MN·s, together with physical constants and the AP/HTPB propellant properties. From the propellant mass fractions,
it computes the equivalent propellant density used in the ballistic model. It then imports the main thermochemical 
quantities obtained from NASA CEA, such as c*, ideal thrust coefficient, chamber temperature, optimal expansion ratio, 
gas density and velocity distributions along the nozzle. These data are used to evaluate nozzle performance and estimate 
friction losses.

The next part fits Vieille's law from the experimental pressure and burning-rate data. The code performs a log-log 
regression to obtain the burning-rate coefficient and pressure exponent, then converts the law into SI units for the 
internal-ballistics calculations. After this, the script defines the BATES burning-surface function and starts the 
main iterative design process. An outer bisection adjusts the initial chamber pressure so that the maximum pressure 
reaches the target value of 70 bar. Inside this loop, the nozzle throat area is sized to satisfy the required thrust, 
including divergence and viscous losses. The grain geometry is then determined through nested bisections on web 
thickness and ri/R, so that the required initial burning area and burning time are both satisfied.

Once the geometry is fixed, the code performs a quasi-steady time integration of the motor firing. At each time step, 
it updates the burnt web thickness, burning surface, chamber pressure and regression rate until burnout. From the final 
time histories, it computes the thrust profile, mass flow rates, propellant mass, total impulse, burning time and 
effective specific impulse. The script then sizes the chamber thermal protection using empirical regression rates 
for the insulation material and estimates the corresponding insulation masses. Finally, it prints a structured summary 
of the results and generates plots showing the Vieille-law fit, nozzle profile, Mach evolution, thrust, pressure, 
burning rate, burning area, mass flow rates and total impulse. An additional final section also estimates the ablative 
protection required for the nozzle regions not covered by the ceramic throat insert.

The combustion chamber thermal protection is sized by dividing the chamber into three zones (forward dome, cylindrical 
wall and aft dome), each with a different empirical erosion regression rate that increases towards the nozzle entrance. 
The insulation material is EPDM with silica filler, and each thickness is computed as the product of local regression 
rate, burning time and a safety factor of 1.5, with a structural minimum of 1.5 mm. Wetted areas are approximated as a 
cylinder with two flat end caps, and the insulation mass per zone follows from density, area and thickness. 
A thin HTPB-based liner is also included between the propellant grain and the EPDM insulation, serving as a 
case-bonding adhesion layer and plasticizer migration barrier; its mass is computed from the total wetted surface and a 
fixed thickness of 1.0 mm.

The nozzle ablative protection covers the convergent and divergent walls outside the ceramic throat insert, which spans 
the region between area ratio 2 on both sides. Each section is modelled as a conical frustum whose wetted area is 
defined by boundary radii and wall slant length. Regression rates are set higher than in the chamber (0.12 mm/s 
convergent, 0.10 mm/s divergent) to account for the greater gas velocities in the nozzle. Thicknesses follow the same 
erosion-based sizing, and the total thermal-protection mass (chamber, liner, plus nozzle) is reported as a fraction of 
the expelled propellant mass.
